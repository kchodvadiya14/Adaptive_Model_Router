> **How to read this.** Generated by `python -m routing_lab.evaluate` (see `backend/routing_lab/`). Interpretation, written after seeing the numbers:
>
> * The learned kNN router adds a small but statistically clear amount over randomly mixing models (AIQ uplift +0.030, CI 0.025 to 0.035). The gateway's original static rules add essentially nothing (+0.004).
> * Large "saving vs the best model" figures are mostly the cheap models being nearly as good: `gpt-4o-mini` alone reaches 95% of `gpt-4o` quality at ~7% of the cost. Credit the router only for savings beyond that and for the AIQ uplift. Against the cheapest single model meeting the same bar the router is roughly 20% cheaper (point estimates from the table below).
> * It does **not** transfer to unseen data sources (0% saving at equal quality with MMLU-Pro or OpenHermes held out), so routers must be trained on each deployment's own traffic.
> * Matching *average* quality can hide losses in one task area (MMLU-Pro drops 0.796 to 0.685 at the matched operating point). Per-segment guards are needed.
> * The router captures little of the oracle headroom (oracle 0.98 quality at $0.43 vs about 0.83 for the router at that cost).
> * SPROUT is a benchmark mixture with 2024-era models and CARROT's list prices, not customer traffic and not this gateway's models. GPQA (94) and MuSR (123) test sets are small. Treat this as evidence the approach works in principle, to be re-measured with replay, shadow mode and a live canary.
